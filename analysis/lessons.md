# Reusable lessons

- The earlier V4-derived V5 lost 7–14–1 to V2 despite positive V3/V4 results. The final V2-derived V5 keeps V2's established movement and wins more often locally against V2, V3, and V4.
- A static visible graph can mislabel a one-step pocket as a dead end when the dragon's own tail vacates after a non-pearl move. A crafted engine match made the initial rescue branch split and die while exact V2 escaped. The final guard checks that specific case.
- More dragons and more total body length do not guarantee a round-limit win; the longest individual dragon can decide it. V5 still loses both Stronghold sides to V2 despite a larger final population.
- Test split safeguards one at a time. Late, leader, and child-exit restrictions did not fix Stronghold; the child-exit restriction also removed a Schooltime win.

## Earlier V4-derived experiment

- Portal memory alone does little when the far exit is outside current vision. An unseen exit can be scored as uncertain, but more portal use did not by itself improve the first V5 experiment. Restricting exploration to poor local routes reduced some regressions; Trophy and Autarky still became worse.
- Allowing a split with an unseen tail can substantially increase unit count without improving match wins. The child's first exit must remain an explicit unknown in the decision and in review.
- Keep V2 as an independent benchmark. The earlier V4-derived candidate beat V4 and V3 more often than it lost on the 11 replay maps, yet lost badly to V2.
- Check both starting sides and exact replay map bytes. Several maps favored one side regardless of the candidate, and same-name sample maps need not have identical bytes.
- Portal-free maps test growth separately from portal changes. Devil, Big Empty, and Default Small were 3–3 combined for the earlier V4-derived candidate versus V4, so that growth change showed no net gain there.
